Dismiss

Purpose of the buddy systemIt is an effective way to help manage the safety of large groups.
         If a person is alone, no one may notice if they go missing or have trouble. With two or more together, someone will notice and be able to help.
          It reinforces the character development trait, provides responsibility, and promotes friendships.
How to do it properlyHave at least one buddy or partner with you when on water or hiking. A buddy team may consist of three Scouts when
necessary, if there is an odd number in a group. When hiking, a minimum group of 4 is required, split into pairs.
        Buddies should know each other and be comfortable together.
       Your buddy should be of similar age, ability (e.g. hiking or swimming ability) and maturity .
Always know where your buddy is and stay close. If your buddy leaves the water, you should leave as well until you are both ready to return.
       Each buddy is responsible for the other.
         If your buddy is having a problem, you must alert the leaders in charge.
Buddy checksWhen swimming, the leader or lifeguard may blow their whistle for a buddy check. All pairs should raise each other's hands to show that
buddies have not split apart.

Paint the Lion Purple
"Paint the Lion Purple" is an annual awareness tradition started by Western Cape Scouts in 2013. Every
Thursday evening during the month of October, Scouts, Cubs, Scouters, and supporters put on their bright
purple Scout t-shirts and scarves to climb Lion's Head in Cape Town. As a sea of purple trickles up the
mountain trail, it "paints" the iconic landmark purple, promoting the Scout brand, celebrating community,
and showing the whole city the pride and energy of the Scouting movement!

A Story of Perseverance

Gather round, Cubs! Sit down on the fynbos, rest your legs, and look down at how far
you’ve climbed today.
Up in these rugged mountains, where the air is fresh and the rocks meet the sky, lives
Blinky, a young Cape Sugarbird with bright yellow feathers and a very long, delicate tail.
Next to him on a sunny rock sits Snort, a chubby, cheerful Baboon Spider with eight hairy
legs.
One cool morning, Blinky and Snort looked up at the very highest crag of Lemoenkop.
"I want to see what the world looks like from up there!" chirped Blinky, flapping his wings.
Snort tapped four of his eight feet in excitement. "I'm coming too! Eight legs are better
than two wings!"
So off they went up the steep, stony trail. At first, it was pure fun. Blinky zipped through the
air, and Snort scampered over the small pebbles. But halfway up, the mountain got
tough. The wind picked up, blowing dust everywhere. Blinky’s tiny wings felt like heavy
sacks of flour, and Snort’s eight little legs were so tired they felt like wobbly noodles.
Just then, a sleek, shadowy Cape Leopard named Luna padded out from behind a
sugarbush. She stretched gracefully and looked down at the two panting little creatures.
"Where are you two struggling off to?" Luna purred softly. "It’s windy, it’s steep, and you
look completely exhausted. Why don't you turn around? There's a nice, quiet shadow
under the bushes down below where you can sleep all afternoon."
Right then, a quiet little voice inside Blinky whispered, "The Leopard is right. My wings hurt. I
want to give up."
And a voice inside Snort whispered, "My eight legs can’t take another step. Let's go sleep
under the bush."
That quiet little voice wasn't the Leopard—it was giving in to yourself. It's the lazy part of
our minds that wants to quit the moment things get hard.
Blinky looked at Snort. Snort looked at Blinky. Snort shook his tiny hairy head and said, "No. I
didn't come half this way just to turn back now."
Blinky took a big breath, tucked his long tail against the wind, and said, "One small flutter
at a time!"
Snort didn't try to dash. He just moved one leg, then the next leg, then the next leg. Blinky
didn't try to fly all the way to the top in one go—he just fluttered to the very next rock.
Hop. Flutter. Step. Every time that lazy little voice whispered "Quit," they pushed it away
and took just one more step together.
And then—Swoosh!
They scrambled over the last rock and stood right on the summit peak! The wind cooled
their hot coats, and beneath them lay the entire ocean, the town, and the valley
stretched out like a gigantic, beautiful map. The lower bushes down below looked so
small, and they realized that if they had listened to that lazy voice, they would have
missed the most amazing view in the world.
Look around you right now, Pack. As you climbed Lemoenkop today, your legs grew tired
and your lungs puffed. That same quiet voice tried to whisper, "It's too steep, let's stop."
But you didn't listen to the lazy voice. You chose perseverance, just like Blinky and Snort.
Remember this summit whenever you face something hard in life. The tired feeling goes
away, but the strength you build by not giving in stays with you forever.
Well done, Cubs! You conquered Lemoenkop!

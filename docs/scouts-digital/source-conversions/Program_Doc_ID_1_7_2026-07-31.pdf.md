```text
Alice and the Clock That Wouldn’t Wait
One sunny afternoon, Alice was sitting beneath a tree when she heard a familiar voice.
“Oh dear! Oh dear! I’m going to be late!”
The White Rabbit rushed past her, wearing his little blue jacket and staring at his pocket watch.
“Late for what?” Alice called.
“For the Queen of Hearts’ garden party!” cried the Rabbit. “It begins at exactly three o’clock!”
Alice looked at the large clock standing near the garden gate.
It was only two o’clock.
“But you have plenty of time,” she said.
“Oh, wonderful!” said the Rabbit. “Then I can stop for a little rest.”
He lay down beneath a mushroom and closed his eyes.
“Just five minutes,” he mumbled.
But five minutes became ten minutes.
Ten minutes became twenty minutes.
And soon, the White Rabbit was snoring.
Alice gently shook his shoulder.
“Wake up! It is half past two!”
The Rabbit jumped into the air.
“Half past two? I still have plenty of time! I shall have a quick cup of tea.”
Alice followed him to the Mad Hatter’s tea table.
The Mad Hatter was pouring tea into a shoe.
The March Hare was balancing biscuits on his nose.
And the Dormouse was asleep inside the sugar bowl.
“Sit down!” cried the Hatter. “There is always time for tea!”
“Only a quick cup,” said the White Rabbit. “I must be at the garden party by three.”
The Hatter filled his cup.
Then he filled it again.
Then he began telling a very long joke about a teapot and a turnip.
Alice glanced at the clock.
“Rabbit,” she warned, “it is quarter to three.”
“Quarter to three!” cried the Rabbit. “I must leave immediately!”
He jumped up, but then noticed a plate of jam tarts.
“Perhaps I have time for just one tart.”
He ate one tart.
Then another.
Then one more, because it was shaped like a heart.
Alice pointed at the clock.
“Five minutes to three!”
The White Rabbit’s ears stood straight up.
“Five minutes!” he squeaked.
He raced through the forest, with Alice running behind him.
They hurried past the talking flowers.
They dashed around the Caterpillar’s mushroom.
They squeezed through a tiny door.
At last, they reached the Queen’s garden.
The great clock struck three.
BONG! BONG! BONG!
The garden gates began to close.
“Wait!” shouted Alice.
She grabbed the White Rabbit’s paw, and together they slipped through just before the gates shut.
The Queen of Hearts stood beside a long table filled with cakes, sandwiches and strawberry juice.
“You are almost late!” she boomed.
The White Rabbit bowed so low that his ears touched the ground.
“I am very sorry, Your Majesty. I thought I had plenty of time.”
The Queen frowned.
“Plenty of time can disappear very quickly.”
Alice nodded.
“If something is important, it is better to arrive a little early than to rush and worry.”
The White Rabbit looked at his watch.
“I understand now,” he said. “Being punctual means being ready and arriving at the right time.”
The Queen smiled.
“Very good. You may stay for the party.”
From that day onwards, the White Rabbit made a new plan.
He prepared everything he needed before he left.
He did not stop for unnecessary naps.
He did not drink three cups of tea when he only had time for one.
And he always tried to arrive a few minutes early.
Strangely enough, once the White Rabbit stopped rushing, he had much more time to enjoy himself.
And whenever someone in Wonderland said, “Don’t worry, we have plenty of time,” the Rabbit would tap his watch and reply:
“Time does not wait, even in Wonderland!”
And he was never late again.
The end.
```

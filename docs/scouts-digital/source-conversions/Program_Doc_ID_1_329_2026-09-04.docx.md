1. Road Safety for Cyclists

We are going to talk about how to stay safe when riding a bicycle on the road.

Always wear a helmet.

Ride on the left side of the road.

Obey traffic lights and road signs.

Use hand signals when turning.

Watch out for cars, people and other cyclists.

Never use your phone while riding.

2. Being Safe and Kind Online

We are going to talk about some words we hear when using the internet and how our online actions can affect others.

Cyberbullying: Being mean or hurting someone online.

Surfing the net: Using the internet to find information or have fun.

Identity theft: Someone stealing your information and pretending to be you.

Social network: An online place where people communicate and share things.

Remember: Think before you post or send something. Don't post or send mean or hurtful messages.

3. Keeping Personal Information Private

Why it is important to keep our personal information safe, especially when talking to people online?

Never give someone you have never met your:

Full name

Address

Phone number

School name

Passwords

Location or photos

If someone you don't know asks for personal information, don't reply. Tell a trusted adult.

1) Know what to do in the case of accidents at home or at your pack meeting

Accidents can happen at home or while we are having fun at our Pack meeting. Today we will learn what to do if someone gets hurt and how we can help safely.

What should I do?

Stay calm and do not panic.

Make sure you are safe before going to help.

Tell an adult or Scouter straight away.

If someone is badly hurt, do not move them unless they are in danger.

Do not try to treat a serious injury yourself.

Follow the instructions of the adult or First Aider.

Know who you can ask for help in an emergency.

Never play with medicines, matches, fire or dangerous objects.

Remember: Stay calm, stay safe and get an adult!

2) Show that you know the basic rules of the road as a pedestrian

We all use roads when walking to school, Pack meetings or when out with our families. Today we will learn how to walk and cross roads safely.

Basic road safety rules:

Always use the pavement/sidewalk when there is one.

Stop before crossing the road.

Look right, left and right again.

Listen carefully for vehicles.

Cross at a pedestrian crossing or traffic light where possible.

Wait for vehicles to stop before crossing.

Walk, don't run, across the road.

Never play or use your phone while crossing.

Never suddenly run into the road.

Hold an adult's hand when crossing if you need help.

Remember: STOP – LOOK – LISTEN – THINK – CROSS!

3) Explain the dangers of talking to strangers

There are many kind and helpful people in the world, but we cannot always know if a person we don't know is safe. Today we will learn how to stay safe when a stranger tries to talk to us.

What should I know?

A stranger is someone you don't know.

Never go anywhere with a stranger.

Never get into a stranger's car.

Don't accept sweets, toys or gifts from a stranger without checking with your trusted adult.

Don't tell strangers where you live or where you go to school.

Don't give a stranger your phone number or other personal information.

If a stranger asks you to keep a secret, tell a trusted adult.

If someone makes you feel uncomfortable or unsafe, move away and find a safe adult.

If necessary, shout “NO!” and run to a safe place.

Remember: Don't go with strangers – get away and tell a trusted adult.

4) Know the importance of saying “NO” to any adult who wants to do something that makes you feel uncomfortable

Sometimes someone may ask us to do something that makes us feel uncomfortable, scared or worried. Today we will learn that it is okay to say “NO”, even to an adult.

Remember:

Your body belongs to you.

You are allowed to say “NO” when something doesn't feel right.

You don't have to hug, kiss or touch someone if you don't want to.

You can move away from someone who makes you uncomfortable.

Use a loud voice and say “NO, STOP!” if you need to.

Tell a parent, caregiver, teacher, Scouter or another trusted adult.

If someone tells you to keep something uncomfortable a secret, tell someone you trust.

If the first person doesn't listen, keep telling trusted adults until someone helps you.

You are never to blame if an adult makes you feel uncomfortable or unsafe.

Remember: It's okay to say NO! Your safety comes first.

5) Know what to do when you are alone at home

Sometimes children may be alone at home for a short time. Today we will learn some important rules to help us stay safe until our parent or caregiver returns.

When I am home alone:

Keep the doors and windows locked.

Don't tell anyone that you are home alone.

Don't open the door for someone you don't know.

If someone knocks, speak through the door or contact your parent/caregiver.

Keep your phone nearby.

Know how to contact a trusted adult.

Never play with matches, candles, the stove or other dangerous things.

Don't use dangerous appliances unless a trusted adult has said it is safe.

If you see something dangerous, stay away and get help.

If you are frightened or there is an emergency, call a trusted adult or emergency services.

Remember: LOCK – DON'T OPEN – STAY SAFE – GET HELP!

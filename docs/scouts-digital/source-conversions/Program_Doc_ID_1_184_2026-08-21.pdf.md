          Setup & Mechanics

             ●   Board Setup: 12 small pits (6 per player) and 2 large end
                      stores (Mancalas). Place 4 beans in each of the 12 pits (48
                            total).
             ●   Objective: Capture and store more beans in your store than
                     your opponent.

          Core Rules

             ●   Sowing: On your turn, pick up all beans from any pit on your
                      side and deposit them one by one counterclockwise into
                     adjacent pits, including your store (skip your opponent's store).
             ●   Free Turn: If your last dropped seed lands directly in your own
                        store, you get an immediate extra turn.
             ●   Capturing: If your last dropped seed lands in an empty pit on
                     your side, you capture that seed along with all beans in the
                    opponent's pit directly opposite, placing them into your store.
             ●  Game End: The game ends as soon as one player's 6 pits are
                     completely empty. The other player gathers all remaining
                  beans on their side and puts them in their store. The player
                      with the most total beans wins

**"The Littlest Roar"**

*A Jungle Yarn for Cubs*


---


Once in the jungle, there was a baby lion cub named Pip.


Pip was the smallest cub in his den. When the other cubs practiced their roars, his came out more like a squeak.


"I'll never roar right," Pip sighed, flopping down in the grass.


Just then, an old wise turtle ambled by. "Young cub, why so glum?"


"My roar is tiny. Everyone else's is big and brave. I'm just... not good enough."


The turtle chuckled softly. "Little one, I've lived in this jungle a long, long time. Do you know what I've learned? Nobody asks the
smallest flower to bloom like the tallest tree. They just ask it to bloom."


"So... I just need a bigger roar?"


"No," said the turtle. "You just need *your* roar — the loudest, bravest one *you* can give. That's all any of us can do."


Pip thought about that. He stood up tall, took the deepest breath his little lungs could hold, and let out the biggest roar he
possibly could.


It still wasn't loud. But it was real. And it was all his.


From that day on, Pip roared his roar — his very best one — every single time. And slowly, day by day, it grew stronger.


Because that's what trying your best does. It doesn't make you the biggest.





It just makes you *you* — growing a little more, every day.


**THE END**

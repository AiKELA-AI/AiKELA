```text
Be prepared, and do your best not to get lost in the first place!
These are things you can do before you head out:

Don’t tell anyone where you’re going.

Before you leave, tell someone where you are going.
Always tell someone where you are going and when you will be back. Make sure that when you do
get back you let them know so that they don't call out the cavalry.

Never take water with you on a hike. You might drown.

Be prepared
Even if you are only going for a day walk, always take a map and a small emergency kit: first aid,
torch (to signal rescuers), matches (to light a signal fire), food and water, warm clothing, a small
mirror for signalling rescuers.

You don’t need to plan your trip. Just use Google.

Plan your expedition
You are much less likely to get lost if you plan your route, study the map before you start, and know
how long it will take. Also check the weather forecast before you go.

You don’t need to keep checking your map. Everything will probably work out if you keep
walking.

Keep track of where you are
Along the way, keep checking the map and taking a mental note of landmarks that you pass. Keep
an eye on the time, and on any changes in the weather.

Split up, so you can cover more ground.

Stick together
Use the buddy system. Don't get separated from the rest of the group. Work as a team.
If you get lost, don't panic - be resourceful!

Run! Maybe you’ll find

Stop - don't push on into the unknown
As soon as you know that you are lost, stop. You are safer staying on a rocky ledge than trying to find
a way down a dangerous cliff. If it is getting dark, it is far better to set up a camp in the remaining
light rather than to stumble on in the dark.

Run around like a headless chicken!

Stay calm, and think!
Don't panic! You are much safer if you stay calm and think clearly.

Find a windy, cold place to stand.

Take shelter
Find shelter or build a shelter to protect you from the elements - wind, cold, rain, heat. Try to build a
fire to keep you warm.
```

## Page 4

[Original page 4](../src/Program_Doc_ID_1_59_2026-06-19.pdf#page=4)

```text
Leave your car, because if it’s not working it can’t help you.

Stay with your vehicle
If you break down in a vehicle, stay with it. It is much easier for a rescuer to find a car than a person
who has wandered away! A vehicle can help in many ways, even if it can't drive.

Sit on your hands and do nothing.

Signal for help
Build a signal fire with lots of damp leaves to ensure that the fire produces a lot of smoke. Make
ground-to-air signals in a clear spot. Shout and blow a whistle when a rescuer is near.

Put spread mud on your face and make sure nobody can spot you.

Be visible
As soon as the sun comes up, try to find an open place where you could be seen from the air.
Stream beds are excellent for this if you are in thick vegetation. Search helicopters generally
traverse waterways first of all when looking for missing people.

Eat all your food, because the extra energy will help you think better.

Make your supplies last
Ration your food and water. Try to make it last as long as you can. If you are low on water, find a
source of water.

Be impatient.

Be patient
If someone knows that you are missing, help is on the way.
```

YARN - The Story the River Told (about 4 minutes)
Once there was a little village built beside a river. Most of the year the river was so quiet that the children hardly noticed it. They crossed
the little bridge on their way to school, families picnicked beside it and cattle drank from the shallow water.
An old woman called Gogo Thandi lived on the highest hill overlooking the village. Whenever the summer clouds began gathering, she
told the children, “Always respect the river. A quiet river can have a very loud story to tell.”
The children thought this was strange. “How can a river tell a story?” they asked. Gogo Thandi smiled. “You have to learn how to listen.”
One afternoon enormous grey clouds filled the sky. The rain began. At first it was wonderful. The children splashed in puddles and
watched little streams run along the road. But the rain did not stop. The little river became wider. Then faster.
One boy, Sipho, ran towards the bridge. “I want to see the flood!” he shouted. His friend Naledi grabbed his arm. “Remember Gogo’s
story. If the river is getting bigger, we go AWAY from it, not towards it.” They hurried uphill.
From the hill they watched branches, rubbish and even part of a fence rush underneath the bridge. Then the bridge disappeared
beneath the muddy water. Sipho stared at it. “I cross there every day,” he whispered. Gogo Thandi nodded. “And today you didn’t.”
The storm eventually passed and the river slowly returned to normal. The next morning the children walked down with the adults. Water
had carved away part of the riverbank. A small shed built close to the river had disappeared. But the houses on higher ground were safe.
Sipho looked at Gogo Thandi. “I think I heard the river’s story.” “What did it say?” she asked. Sipho thought. “It said that water is stronger
than I am.” Naledi added, “And being curious isn’t worth getting hurt.”
Gogo smiled. “And what will you do the next time the river begins telling that story?” Every child answered together: “Move away from
the water, get to a safe place and tell an adult.”
From that day on, whenever clouds gathered over the village, the children did not just watch the rain. They listened to the story the river
was telling.
National Challenge / Scouter Notes

  • Flood in a Tray: Cubs build and flood their landscape, observe the effects, make improvements and test it again.
  • Water Safety Awareness: Create a poster raising awareness on water safety and present it to Meerkats or your local ECD. Friday’s 1-minute
 Pack presentations are rehearsal; keep the posters so the actual Meerkat/ECD presentation can still be completed.
  • Keep the water quantity and pouring point the same for Test 1 and Test 2 so the Cubs can compare their changes fairly.

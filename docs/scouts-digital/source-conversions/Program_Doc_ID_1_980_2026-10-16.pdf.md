```text
Badge Requirement # 1: What plants are best suited to our area? (Highveld)

Johannesburg is on the Highveld, a high, mostly flat area that is naturally a grassland region. We
experience warm, wet summers, and cold, dry winters with frost, and we need to choose plants that
grow well in these conditions.

Choosing plants and trees that grow naturally in our area, also called indigenous plants, will help:
-conserve our precious water resources,
-protect soil structure,, attract birds, butterflies and other creatures to our garden and other green
spaces, and,
-preserve wildlife that is struggling to adapt and thrive in our rapidly urbanising area.

We need a variety of plants including flowering plants, grasses, succulents, ground covers, shrubs, and
trees. When choosing these plants, we must consider what kind of temperature and rainfall they need.

Many succulents are from areas that have hot, dry summers and wet winters, like Cape Town, and these
plants will rot in Johannesburg’s wet summers and struggle in our cold, dry winters. Certain aloes and
vygies grow very well here; a book for our local conditions, a local garden centre, and the internet
could help us choose wisely.

Many plants and trees can’t tolerate frost. If we want to plant these, we need to carefully choose a
protected area in our gardens, or choose plants that are better suited to cold, dry, frosty winters.

Different kinds of grasses are good to include in our gardens because Johannesburg is naturally part of
a grassland area. This doesn’t mean that big, lush green lawns are best! Lawns use a lot of water,
require maintenance like trimming and mowing adds to air pollution (electric and petrol lawnmowers
both cause air and noise pollution), and require unnatural fertilisers that pollute and poison precious
groundwater sources. If we want a lawn, we should ask the advice of a landscaper who knows and
cares about our local climate conditions, find out ways to water it wisely and fertilise it naturally, and
could also consider decreasing the size of our lawns to include more space to grow flowers, shrubs,
trees and even food. There is even a a book called ‘FOOD NOT LAWNS’ that encourages people to grow
food rather than environmentally unfriendly lawns!

Some examples of plants that grow well here are:

Spekboom
Mountain aloes
Agapanthus
Strelitzia (also called the Bird of Paradise)
Wild Garlic
The “hen and chicken” plant, also called the “spider plant” grows well under trees and in shady areas

Sweet potato vines make excellent ground covers, need very little water, can tolerate full, hot sun, and
we can eat the leaves as well as the roots.
Herbs like rosemary and thyme attract bees, have low water needs and high tolerance for full sun, and
have medicinal and culinary uses.

For trees, the magnificent white stinkwood is a good choice for a space that can take a large tree,
while the karee, wild olive, and yellowwood are smaller trees that grow well in our climate.

We should always try grow some FOOD, and in summer tomatoes, peppers, pumpkins and squash as well
as most herbs grow very well, while in winter we can grow beautiful cabbages, broccoli, cauliflower,
spinach and Swiss chard, leeks and onions. Many fruit trees grow well with a little care too, like citrus
(oranges, lemons, limes), avocado, and peach trees.
```

```text
Badge Requirement # 2: What do plants need to grow?

The Essential Needs of Plants


  •   Light: Plants use light energy (from the sun or special grow lights) for
     photosynthesis, the process of turning carbon dioxide and water into food
      (glucose).

  •   Water: Roots absorb water from the soil. Water carries vital minerals through
     the plant and keeps the plant firm and upright (turgor pressure).

  •    Air: Plants take in carbon dioxide (CO₂) through their leaves to make food,
     and they use oxygen (O₂) in their roots to breathe (respiration).

  •   Nutrients: Plants pull essential minerals like nitrogen, phosphorus, and
     potassium from the soil to build leaves, grow strong roots, and fight disease.

  •   Space: Plants need room above and below ground so their roots can spread
     out and their leaves can reach the light without crowding each other

  •   Temperature: Plants need warmth to grow and thrive. Many plants enter a
     period of dormancy during winter, when they lose their leaves and almost
    seem to die, but when the temperature starts rising in early spring, new
     leaves flowers, and fruit emerge from the woody parts of the plant. These
      plants are called “deciduous plants”.
```

```text
Badge Requirement # 7: How to attract Birds to your garden

To attract birds to your garden, you need to provide four key things:
food, water, shelter, and a safe environment.

1. Provide Diverse Food Sources

   •   Seeds and Grains

   •   Suet and Mealworms: Hang suet blocks or offer dried mealworms to attract insect-eating
       birds like robins, starlings, thrushes, and woodpeckers.

   •   Nectar and Fruit: Plant red, orange, and yellow indigenous flowers (like aloes, wild dagga,
     and red-hot pokers) for nectar-feeding birds, or put out fresh fruit like over-ripe apples and
       berries for thrushes

2. Offer Clean Water

   •   Shallow Bird Baths: Provide a wide, shallow bird bath (no deeper than 2 to 3 inches) with
      a rough surface or stones so birds can grip and stand safely.

   •   Moving Water: Add a dripper, bubbler, or small fountain. The sound and movement of
       rippling water attract birds from a distance and signal that it is fresh.

   •   Maintenance: Change the water daily and scrub the container weekly to stop algae
      growth and prevent the spread of disease

3. Create Natural Shelter and Nesting Sites

   •   Layered Plants: Design your garden with a mix of tall trees, medium shrubs, and lower
      ground covers. Dense, evergreen shrubs offer year-round protection from weather and
       predators.

   •   Leave Wild Areas: Keep a corner of your garden a bit messy by piling up fallen leaves,
       logs, and compost. This creates a natural habitat for insects, worms, and grubs, which
      feed many local bird species.

   •   Nest Boxes: Install wooden nest boxes for cavity-nesting birds, facing them north or east
       to protect them from harsh midday sun and driving rain

4. Ensure Safety

   •   Avoid Chemicals: Stop using chemical pesticides, herbicides, and slug pellets. These kill
        off the natural insects birds need to eat and can poison the birds directly.

   •   Manage Pets: Keep cats indoors—especially at dawn, dusk, and night—or fit them with a
        bell to give birds an early warning.

   •   Protect Windows: Position feeders either very close to windows (within 3 feet) or far away
      (more than 30 feet) so birds do not build up fatal speed if they fly toward the glass
        reflection.
```

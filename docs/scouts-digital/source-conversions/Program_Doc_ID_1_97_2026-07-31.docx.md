Lifeboat Relay – "Abandon Ship!"

Story

"There's a huge storm at sea! Your ship has hit rocks and is sinking. Your whole crew must get safely to the island, but you only have one lifeboat. Can your crew work together to save everyone before the ship disappears?"

Equipment

One hula hoop (or a large rope tied into a circle) per Six – this is the lifeboat.

Two cones about 30 metres apart.

Optional: beanbags or a teddy to represent "supplies" or an injured sailor.

Set Up

One cone is the sinking ship.

The other cone is the safe island.

Each Six stands behind the starting cone with their lifeboat.

How to Play

The lifeboat can only carry two Cubs at a time.

The first two Cubs stand inside the hula hoop (or hold the rope circle around them).

They walk or jog together to the island, keeping the lifeboat around them.

One Cub stays safely on the island.

The other Cub returns with the lifeboat to collect another sailor.

Continue until the whole Six has been rescued.

The first team with everyone safely on the island wins.

Challenge Rules

To make it more exciting:

The lifeboat must stay touching both sailors.

No running.

If the lifeboat falls off or someone steps out, they must return to where they started.

Cubs must encourage one another—good teamwork earns bonus points.

Harder Version (Perfect for Gold Wolves)

Halfway through the rescue, shout:

"A storm is coming!"

Now the lifeboat must carry:

a beanbag (food supplies),

or a teddy (an injured sailor),

without dropping it.

This suddenly forces the Cubs to slow down and cooperate even more.

Yarn: The Sailor Who Didn't Like Knots

"Tonight we're going to meet a young sailor named Tom. Tom loved everything about the sea. He loved the salty air.

He loved dolphins racing alongside the ship. He loved climbing the mast to look for land. But there was one thing Tom hated...

Practising knots!

Whenever the captain handed him a piece of rope and said,'Tom, let's practise the reef knot,' Tom would sigh. 'Again? I already know it!' The captain smiled and replied,

'Knowing about a knot isn't the same as being able to tie it.'Tom thought the captain worried far too much.

One sunny morning the ship set sail across a calm blue ocean. The sea was as smooth as glass, and everyone was happy. Tom thought,

'See? We don't need all these knots. Everything is easy.'

Has anyone here ever thought something was easy... until it suddenly wasn't?

(Allow a few Cubs to answer.)

Well, later that afternoon, dark clouds rolled across the sky. The wind began to howl. The waves grew bigger and bigger. The ship rocked from side to side. Then...

SNAP!

One of the ropes holding the mainsail broke free. The great sail flapped wildly in the wind. The captain shouted,

'Tom! Secure that rope with a reef knot!'

Tom grabbed the rope. His hands shook. He wrapped it once... Then twice... No... Was it over first? Or under? He simply couldn't remember.

The sail kept whipping through the air. The whole ship was in danger. Then an older sailor named Ben rushed over. In just a few seconds he tied a perfect reef knot. The sail was secure. The ship steadied.

Everyone was safe.

That evening the storm had passed. The captain called Tom over. He wasn't angry. He simply handed Tom a piece of rope. 'Tomorrow the sea may be calm again,' he said. 'But sailors don't practise on stormy days.

They practise on calm days... so they are ready when the storm comes.'

Tom nodded. From that day on he practised every evening. While the others chatted after supper, Tom tied knots. He tied them with his eyes open. Then with his eyes closed. He tied them behind his back. He even challenged himself to see how quickly he could tie them correctly. At first it took him a whole minute. Soon it took thirty seconds.

Then twenty.

Before long he could tie every knot without even thinking. Years passed. Tom became one of the best sailors in the fleet. Eventually he became the captain of his own ship.

Whenever a young sailor complained, 'Why do we have to keep practising these knots?' Captain Tom would smile and hand them a rope.

Then he would say,

'One day someone might depend on the knot you tie.

Practise now, so you're ready when that day comes.'

Discussion

Ask the Cubs:

Why do sailors need good knots?

Which knot do you think would be the most useful on a boat?

Why do we keep practising skills at Cubs, even when we think we already know them?

Closing Thought

"Every Cub learns knots for the same reason sailors do—not because ropes are exciting, but because one day those skills might help you solve a problem, help a friend, or even save someone's life. The best time to learn a skill is before you need it."

This story fits beautifully with your Plain Sailing theme and naturally leads into the knot activities for both the Silver Wolves and Gold Wolves. It also reinforces the Cub Scout values of perseverance, preparedness, and helping others.

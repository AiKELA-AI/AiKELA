## Page 3

[Original page 3](../src/Program_Doc_ID_1_42_2026-05-22.pdf#page=3)

```text
Base 1
If YOUR clothes
   1. stay calm
   2. Stop, drop, and roll
      Stop
            •  Stop running immediately.
            •  Running makes the fire burn bigger because of the air.
     Drop
            •  Drop to the ground quickly.
            •  Cover your face with your hands if you can.
       Roll
            •   Roll over and over to smother the flames.
            •  Keep rolling until the fire is out.
   3. Call for help
    •  Shout for an adult or emergency help.

If SOMEONE ELSE’S clothes are on fire
     1.  Get an adult immediately.
     2. AFTER the person has rolled out most of the flames and if it is SAFE FOR
     YOU:
        •  A fire blanket
        •  A heavy coat
        •  A towel
         to help smother the flames.

DO NOT:
   •  Throw any water or anything on the person (you don’t know how it
      started so you will make things worse)
   •  Chase after the person (that may cause them to panici, just shout stop
     drop and roll)
   • DO NOT PUT ANYTHING ON THE BURNS EXCEPTE COOL RUNNING
    WATER TILL HELP ARRIVES
```

```text
Base 2
Why:
The recovery position:
    •  Keeps the airway open
    •  Helps them breathe safely
    •  Stops choking if they are sick or vomit

When TO put someone in recovery position:
   •  unconscious or not responding BUT STILL breathing normally

Always check if the person is responding (call out to them and give them a
slight shake)

Wen NOT TO put someone in recovery position:
   •  unconscious or not responding AND NOT breathing normally (CPR)
   •  You think they may have a serious neck or back injury
```

```text
Base 3
CPR
   •  Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation
   •  Used when the person is unconscious or not responding AND NOT
      breathing normally
   •  Oxygen filled blood moving around the body until help arrives

   •  Help arrives/a more advanced first aider takes over
     The person starts breathing
     You are unable to continue due external dangers/physically too
     exhausted (about to collapse)




30 compressions to 2 breaths (DO NOT DO BREATHS UNLESS YOU HAVE A
MOUTHPIECE)
Breaths only extremally important in drowning
Only do compressions
Rorate after your 30
4 – 5 cm deep compressions (not uncommon to break/crack ribs)
```

PLAN:-

It is important to allow the Cubs to repeat the PLAN with the key words.  Ask them to remember the PLAN when they next plan an outing near water.  Remind them that accidents happen when they don’t have a PLAN in place and when there’s no PLAN, there’s lot of PANIC when things go wrong.  They must know how to turn PANIC into a PLAN.

P – prepare/pack

Swimming costume

Towels

Food

Water to drink

L – look

Waves

Deep water

Currents eg.  rip current

Slippery rocks

Deep mud

Test the water for depth

Signs or flags (warning you if it is dangerous to enter the water)

REMEMBER to look out for any water around the home.  Cover buckets, toilets and water storage tanks.  Always close doors and gates to stop small children from wandering outside alone.

Rip Currents:-

A rip current is like a river of water moving fast out to see.  It is formed by water returning to its natural level following a build up near the beach.  Waves bring water to the beach and sometimes this forms a rip as the water needs to get back out behind the waves.  The rip current forms when the retreating water concentrates in a channel.  The larger the surf, the stronger the rip current will be.

Rip currents can be recognized by:

Rippled, darker appearance of the water

Waves breaking on each side of what looks like a relatively calm, dark colour, water channel – that looks like a river

Discolouration due to sand particles carried in the water

Helpful tips about rip currents:

Never swim alone.

Be cautious at all times.  Always swim at a beach where there is a lifeguard on duty and swim between their flags.

Obey all instructions from lifegaurds.

If caught in a rip current, remain calm to conserve energy and think clearly, DO NOT PANIC.

Don’t fight the current.  Swim out of the current in a direction following the shoreline (parallel to the beach).  When out of the current, swim towards shore.

If you are unable to swim out of the rip current, you should draw attention to yourself by facing the shore and wave your arms and SHOUT for help.

If you see anyone it trouble, you should get help from a lifeguard or phone 112.

You can throw something that floats into the see and point for the person to swim to the side, out of the current.  But you should not go into the water.

A – ask an adult

Emphasise this is not negotiable.  They MUST ask an adult if they want to go and swim.

They need to find a responsible adult to watch them when they swim.

Make sure that they tell a responsible adult exactly where they are going, when they will return and the route that they will take there and back so they know where to find them in the case of an emergency.

Explain that many people drown because they didn’t tell anyone that they would be swimming, so no one could come to their rescue when they got into trouble.

This rule applies to adults who go swimming too (even when they are good swimmers).  We always make sure someone is watching us and that they know WHO to call for help.

N – never

Never swim alone; use the “buddy system”.

Never swim in unknown waters (that is in dams, rivers, or pools you do not know).

Never dive into water if it is not clear or if you do not know how deep it is.  You could hit something and hurt yourself.

Never swim with your clothes on – they get heavy.

Never swim and drink alcohol.  Also, never swim when supervised by adults that have been drinking/are drinking alcohol as their response time to help will be impaired.

Never swim when you are too anything eg. too tired, too hungry, too cold, too upset

Never run around the pool, river or dam.  ALWAYS WALK.

Never push people into a pool, river or dam, dive without first looking or duck your friends.

Never eat or chew while swimming.

Never use your hand to rescue a friend.

Never jump in to save your friend – call for help!

Never leave a child alone when they are near water (MOST important “never” for adults).

Never be distracted by your cell phone while working with children in or near water.

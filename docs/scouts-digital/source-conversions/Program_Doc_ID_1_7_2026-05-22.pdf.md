```text
Getting lost in the veld: STOP
S – Stop
As soon as you realise you are lost, stop walking. Do not run, wander, or “just try one more
path”. Moving around makes it harder for adults or rescue helpers to find you. Take a few
slow breaths and stay calm.
T – Think
Ask yourself: “Where was the last place I saw the group?” “Can I hear voices, traffic, water,
or a whistle? “Is there a safe open space nearby?” “What do I have with me: water, whistle,
jacket, hat?”
Do not eat wild plants, berries, mushrooms, or unknown fruit. Do not drink from streams
unless an adult has told you it is safe or the water has been treated.
O – Observe
Look around carefully without moving far. Check for landmarks such as a hill, fence, path,
river, big rock, gate, power line, or building. Also observe the weather, shade, wind, insects,
animals, and whether there is any fire or smoke nearby. Choose a safe waiting place: visible,
open, sheltered from extreme sun or wind, and away from cliffs, rivers, animal holes, thick
bush, and burnt or burning areas.
```

```text
P – Plan
Make a simple safety plan: Stay where you are unless there is immediate danger. Make
yourself visible with bright clothing, a hat, backpack, or scarf. Use your whistle or voice in
bursts: three blasts/shouts is a common distress signal. Stay warm or cool depending on the
weather. Conserve energy and water. Wait for help.
A good rule is: “Hug a tree” or stay next to a clear landmark. It gives you a safe place to wait
and makes you easier to find.

Natural dangers in the veld:

Weather and temperature
The veld can get very hot during the day and cold later, especially if the weather changes.
Heat can cause dehydration, sunburn, and heat exhaustion; cold, wet, and wind can cause
chilling. Hiking safety guidance also warns about fatigue, hypothermia, dehydration, and heat
exhaustion as real hiking risks.
Prevention: wear a hat, use sunscreen, drink water, carry a warm top or rain jacket, and stay
with the group.

Veld fires
Dry grass, wind, and heat can make veld fires spread quickly. SCOUTS South Africa’s Safety
Code badge specifically expects Scouts to understand the dangers of veld fires and
precautions when camping or hiking.
Prevention: never play with matches or lighters, only make fires where adults say it is safe,
and report smoke or flames immediately.

Snakes, insects, spiders, ticks, and scorpions
Most creatures will avoid people, but they may bite or sting if stepped on, picked up, or
trapped.
Prevention: watch where you walk, do not put hands into holes or under rocks, shake out
shoes, wear closed shoes, and tell an adult if bitten or stung.

Thorny plants and poisonous plants
The veld may have thorns, burrs, stinging plants, and plants that are unsafe to eat.
Prevention: do not eat wild plants unless a knowledgeable adult says they are safe, and do
not push through thick bush unnecessarily.

Rivers, streams, dams, and flash floods
Water can look shallow but be slippery, fast, or deeper than expected. If a river is in flood
and dangerous, the safe choice is to wait rather than cross.
Prevention: never cross water without adult instruction, and stay away from flooded streams.

Rocks, holes, slopes, and uneven ground
Loose stones, animal burrows, dongas, cliffs, and slippery ground can cause falls or sprains.
Prevention: walk, do not run; stay on the path; and look where you put your feet.

Larger animals and livestock
Cattle, dogs, baboons, warthogs, antelope, and other animals can be dangerous if surprised,
cornered, or protecting young.
Prevention: keep your distance, do not feed animals, and never chase or tease them.
```

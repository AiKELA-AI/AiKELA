```text
Notes:

Pack Scouters/ Helpers:

Game: “First Aid Freeze”
How it works:
•  Cubs run or skip around the hall/field while leaders call out accident scenarios (e.g., “Cut on the knee!”, “Nosebleed!”, “Burn on the hand!”).
•  When they hear the scenario, Cubs must freeze and act out the correct first aid response with their bodies:
      o  Cut/graze → pretend to wash and cover the wound.
      o  Nosebleed → sit down, lean forward, pinch nose.
      o  Burn/scald → hold hand under imaginary running water.
      o  Bleeding → press hand firmly on “wound.”
      o  Emergency call → hold hand to ear like a phone and say “Help!”
   Scouter:
•   Call out different scenarios quickly to keep energy high.
•  Mix in “safe” calls like “All clear!” — Cubs unfreeze and run again.
•   Praise correct actions and gently correct mistakes.

SW Activities
•  Summoning help: Role-play accident scenes; Cubs shout “Help Akela!” or call for adult.
•  Cuts/grazes: Demonstrate cleaning with water, applying plaster.
•  Nosebleed: Sit forward, pinch nose, stay calm.
•  Emergency kit: Cubs collect items (plasters, wipes, gloves, torch, whistle). – Will speak about the things to collect
•   Sling: Practice with scarf/triangular bandage.

  GW Activities
•  Emergency behaviour: Stay calm, don’t crowd, call for help.
•  EMS activation: Practice dialling local emergency number, explain clearly.
•  Burns/scalds: Cool with water, cover lightly.
•   Bites/stings: Wash, apply cold compress; tweezers for splinter removal.
•   Direct pressure: Use clean cloth/bandage, press firmly until bleeding stops.
```

```text
The Pack Helps Each Other
The sun was dipping low behind the Seeonee Hills when the Pack gathered in the clearing. The cubs were
lively, chasing one another, until Akela raised his head and called them close.

“Tonight,” he said, “we remember that every wolf cub must be ready to help. A careless paw may cause
harm, but a careful paw can save a life.”

Raksha stepped forward, carrying a small pouch. She opened it to show bandages, cloth, and a scarf. “Even
the smallest cub can help,” she said. “When someone is hurt, the first thing we do is call for an elder.
Summoning help is the bravest step of all.”

Bagheera padded silently to the circle. “But sometimes,” he added, “help begins with us. A cut or graze can
be cleaned and covered. A nosebleed can be calmed with gentle hands. A scarf can become a sling to
support a tired arm. These are simple things, but they show great care.”

Mowgli remembered when his friend in the village had fallen. He had shouted for the healer, but while waiting
he pressed a cloth to the wound and kept his friend calm. “I was afraid,” he admitted, “but I knew that
helping—even in small ways—made a difference.”

Akela’s eyes gleamed. “And when danger is greater, the Pack must stay calm. Burns cooled with water,
stings soothed, bleeding stopped with firm pressure—these are the ways we protect each other. To panic is
to forget the Law. To act with courage is to live the Promise.”

The cubs lifted their heads proudly. They saw that first aid was not only about bandages and kits, but about
being steady, calling for help, and caring for one another.

“The strength of the Pack is the Wolf,” Akela said, “and the strength of the Wolf is the Pack. When each cub
learns to help, the Pack is safe, and every wolf cub grows strong.”
```

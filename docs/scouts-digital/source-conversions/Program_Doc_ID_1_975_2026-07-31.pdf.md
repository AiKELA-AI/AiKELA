Milo and the Day of Noticing

Milo was a curious little boy with hair that stuck up like the bristles of a paintbrush. He loved running,
jumping, and playing, but he didn’t always notice the small things around him. One sunny morning, his
grandmother handed him a small notebook and a pencil.

“Today,” she said with a twinkle in her eye, “I want you to be a detective. Your mission is to notice five
things you’ve never seen before.”

Milo grinned. “Easy!” he thought.

He stepped outside and began his search. At first, he saw the same old street, the same old trees, and the
same old houses.

But then, he slowed down. He crouched low and noticed tiny ants marching in a perfect line, each carrying a
crumb bigger than its head. That was his first discovery.

Next, he spotted a crack in the sidewalk shaped exactly like a lightning bolt. He sketched it in his notebook.
His second discovery.

By the park, he heard a sound he’d never paid attention to before—the soft plop of a frog jumping into the
pond. He wrote it down as his third.

Under a big oak tree, he saw a squirrel burying an acorn, its little paws moving quickly like it was hiding
treasure. Fourth discovery.

Finally, as he headed home, he noticed the way the sunlight made the windows of his house sparkle like
gold. That was his fifth.

When Milo showed his grandmother the notebook, she smiled. “You see, the world is full of wonders, but
you have to slow down to find them.”

That night, Milo placed the notebook under his pillow. He decided that tomorrow, and every day after, he
would be a detective of the little things—because sometimes, the smallest details hold the biggest magic.

# The Newspaper Kite

## Type

Handcraft

## Equipment

This kite has no rigid sticks used in its construction, instead you use rolled up newspaper, so if you have an old newspaper, a roll of sellotape, something for the tail and some scissors you have everything you need!

## Instructions

See above. | [Original visual unavailable in this checkout; consult the linked original source.] Fold in half using page crease – so the paper is taller than it is wide. | [Original visual unavailable in this checkout; consult the linked original source.] Make a mark 15 cm in from the edge on both the top (point A) and bottom (point B) of the folded paper. From the top corner, also make a mark 15 cm down the edge (point C). See above. Draw a line on the top edge connecting the points A-C and C-B. Cut along these lines. | [Original visual unavailable in this checkout; consult the linked original source.] Open up the paper. Using tape, tape along the edges and on the inside as shown above. This is to keep the kite strong so it won’t tear while in the air. |

| [Original visual unavailable in this checkout; consult the linked original source.] Now you need to make two supports for the kite. Using another full piece of paper, tightly roll it into a tube. Use a little bit of tape to help keep it rolled up. Do this twice. [Original visual unavailable in this checkout; consult the linked original source.] KITE FLYER’S CODE I will fly kites in an open field, away from electric power lines and transmission towers. I will use dry string, never wire. (Wire could attract and conduct lightning. I won’t use anything metal in my kites. I will not try to remove a kite if it catches in an electric power line or on a high pole. I will not fly kites on or near public roads or highways. I will never fly a kite in wet or stormy weather. That only invites lightning. | [Original visual unavailable in this checkout; consult the linked original source.] Now tape these tubes to the kite along the lines indicated in the above diagram. If the rolls are too long, cut them to size. | [Original visual unavailable in this checkout; consult the linked original source.] Next you need to make a hole in each side of the kite at the side points and at the bottom. A paper hole punch works well. You should be punching through tape so the hole doesn’t rip. Then you need to cut two pieces of string, the first one a metre and a half long. Tie the first one across the widest point of the kite. In the middle of the first one make a loop; this is the bridle that will allow you to attach the kite to your string. The second piece of string can be about one metre long. Tie this between the two points at the bottom. Attach three to five metres of something light to act as a tail; chevron tape or streamers work well. | [Original visual unavailable in this checkout; consult the linked original source.] Go fly the kite! You can experiment with the length of the first piece of string along the widest part of the kite; tie the knot to shorten the midpoint and see what effect it has on how the kite flies. You can also experiment by adding a crosspiece of rolled paper between the two spars on the kite; does it help or hinder the kite? How does moving it at different points up and down the middle affect how the kite flies? |

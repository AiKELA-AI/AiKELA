# In the …, Out of the …

## Type

ACTIVE GAME

## Equipment

String, masking tape, or chalk

## Instructions

Mark a long line on the ground with string or masking tape. Have the Cubs stand along one side, facing the leader. Explain which side is “In the [place]” and which is “Out of the [place]”. When the leader calls one of these commands, the Cubs jump across the line to the matching side. The leader can point one way while calling the other, repeat a command, or vary the pace to make the game more challenging.

Choose a pair of names that suits the meeting theme. For example:

- “In the tomb!” / “Out of the tomb!”
- “In the river!” / “Out of the river!”
- “In the river!” / “On the bank!”
- “On the road!” / “Off the road!”
- “In the grass!” / “Out of the grass!”
- “In the pool!” / “Out of the pool!”
- “In the jungle!” / “Out of the jungle!”

The line represents the boundary; do not play this beside a real road, pool or river. For a competitive version, anyone who jumps to the wrong side or fails to move on a changed command sits out. The last Cub still playing wins. For a non-elimination version, keep everyone in and see who can follow the calls correctly for the longest.

**Review:** The Pyramids source gives the tomb wording. The Herd Boy programme gives the team variation below. The other place-name examples above are adaptations for reuse in different programme themes.

### Herd Boy team variation

The Herd Boy programme describes a team version using two chalk lines to mark the river. Divide Cubs into two teams and have them line up on one side. Call “in the river” or “on the bank”; Cubs jump to the matching zone. The source also uses “on the river” and “in the bank” as trick calls. A Cub who reacts incorrectly moves to the other team. The team with the most Cubs at the end wins. The source does not specify what happens when a Cub makes an ordinary mistake, so agree that rule before starting.

# Landscape-map woodcraft signs

## Type

Work session — user-supplied activity, adapted with the sourced paired trail-sign practice below.

## Equipment

Hand-drawn landscape maps and small stones supplied by the Pack leader; trays for stones; the SSA Cub Pro-Plan Chart 12, page 1. Add small twigs only if available; stones can make the shapes on the chart.

## Instructions

In pairs, Cubs place woodcraft signs on a landscape map to describe a route for someone else to follow. Use the sign chart to demonstrate each sign and its meaning first. One Cub lays signs while the other explains them, then reverse roles. Exchange maps with another pair: the readers trace the route with a finger, explaining the signs as they go, while its makers listen without prompting. Discuss unclear arrangements and let the makers improve them. These turn-taking and checking steps are adaptations of paired practice, not additional SSA requirements.

Choose a start and destination on the existing map. Keep signs clear of one another and distinguish signs from landscape features. A themed destination may represent the object of a search; retain the real meanings of the signs. All routes stay on the maps. A suggested extension is a tabletop route with 20 sign placements, using repeated signs where needed; this is practice, not a claim to complete Gold Wolf's actual trail requirement.

## Preparation and safety

Keep stones on tables or trays, away from mouths, and clear spills promptly. Do not throw stones. Use rounded stones without sharp edges. Permit a Cub to direct a partner to place signs if manipulating small stones is difficult. Use the selected Pack's own reward system.

## Advancement / badge link

The Cub Trail, November 2019, page 13: Silver Wolf Trails requires “Know how to make and recognise at least ten woodcraft signs.” and “Follow a woodcraft trail.” Gold Wolf Trails requires “Lay a woodcraft trail where 20 signs are used for others to follow.” This map exercise supports making/recognising practice; it does not establish that either actual-trail requirement is met. Chart 12 shows five named meanings with material variations; do not claim those variations establish ten distinct signs or automatically award sign-off.

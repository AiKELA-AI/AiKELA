## Instructions

At 13 B-P won a scholarship to Charterhouse School. “I was not a clever boy, nor, I grieve to say, was I as industrious boy as I ought to have been. According to the school reports I began fairly well in my conduct but deteriorated as I went on.” Nevertheless the Headmaster saw promise in him and later on, when he found it necessary to work hard, this encouraged him.

Fascinated by a long stretch of woodland called “The Copse” bordering on the playing fields, but outside the school walls and therefore out of bounds, B-P used often to steal out there and imagine himself a backwoodsman, trapper and scout.

He set snares and occasionally caught a rabbit. He learned by painful experience to skin, clean and cook it but, knowing enemies lurked in the shape of masters looking for boys out of bounds, B-P used very small non-smoking fires. He used to hide up in the trees when masters came, for experience showed that people seldom looked upward when walking. This was the time when he started noticing small details and making deductions. He used to creep about warily looking for ‘signs’ and observing at close range rabbits, squirrels, rats and birds.

The Headmaster liked him and reported that “his ability is greater than would appear by the results of his school work and I am very well satisfied with his conduct.” The Headmaster thought play-acting a very useful form of education and encouraged all the boys to take part. Although not keen at first, B-P found it good fun and in later life it helped him when he had to make unrehearsed speeches. Later, when he was sent out spying, he was able to disguise his features and voice so well that even his own friends did not recognize him.

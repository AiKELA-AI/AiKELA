# The Village Sound Makers

## Type

Yarn

## Equipment

None

## Instructions

Read aloud:

In a small village, the children were getting ready for a welcome evening. Everyone was busy sweeping, setting out chairs and hanging paper decorations. But something was missing: the musicians’ instruments had not arrived.

“The guests will be here tomorrow,” said Nandi. “How will we welcome them without music?”

Sam sat quietly. Outside, he heard the wind rustle the leaves and rain tap on the roof. Inside, someone moved a chair: scrape. A box shifted on the floor: thump.

“There are sounds all around us,” said Lulo. Nandi listened. “Yes, but a sound is not yet a tune. Can we find a way to bring sounds together?”

The children looked around the hall. They had a problem, but the evening was getting late. They promised to meet again in the morning and try to find an answer.

Ask: “What problem did the children face?” “What sounds did they notice?” “How might they welcome the guests?”

### Optional linking snippets

Use these short passages between programme activities. Each advances the story from the unresolved problem in the yarn; they are not needed if the yarn or activities are not used.

**Before the children make instruments:**

The next morning, Nandi returned to the hall. On a table she found clean boxes and elastic bands left over from packing. Sam tapped the table. Could ordinary things help them answer last night’s question? Let’s see what the children can make.

**Before exploring the new instruments:**

A gentle pluck made the first sound. Sam stopped and listened closely. Would every band sound the same? The children took turns trying their new instruments.

**Before the Sixes create a sound pattern:**

Before the children could decide which sounds belonged together, the village bell rang: the guests were on their way. Each Six needed a short pattern they could play together before the doors opened.

**After the performances:**

The guests tapped along and cheered. The children thanked one another for listening, taking turns and sharing their sounds. Nandi smiled: “Each of us brought one small sound, and together we made a welcome.”

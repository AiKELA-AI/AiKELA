| Yarn: | The Water Barrel |
| --- | --- |

“David, Father and I are leaving you one thing to attend to this morning. Fill the water barrel, so that it will last all tomorrow.” “I am going down to the meadow to help Peter with the sheep,” said David. “No. Bring up the water first, or else it may never get done. After that, you can do as you like. We shall be home at six o’clock. Good-bye. ”The wheels rolled down the lane. David walked to the kitchen. He was proud of the house, which was all new and not quite finished. It had been built specially for them. The well was not drilled yet; they were going to have pipes laid. His job was to keep the barrel over the sink filled from the spring. Then the tap was turned on by Mother. David climbed up to see how much water was in it, and found he couldn’t touch the surface with his fingertips. He sighed and got the bucket from under the sink, thinking of all the other things he wanted to do. He got five buckets full, and poured the water in; then he sat down to rest. “Why wouldn’t the rest do in the afternoon?”

David started down the path to the field, then remembered last time Mother had gone to town he had promised to fill the barrel for washing-day, he hadn’t done it and she couldn’t wash because Father, Peter and David had gone to haul timber so there was no one to fetch water. He got five more buckets. “I’ll call that enough.” But the barrel was still only half-full. “Two more and I’ll finish.” It suddenly occurred to David what fun it would be to fill the barrel for once really entirely full. He started with fresh enthusiasm. It took sixteen buckets to fill it right up to the brim. Then he went out to Peter, working in the field. An hour later, Peter stopped digging and looked at the house in the distance. “David! Look at that smoke, where is it coming from?” he cried. David mounted the fence. “It seems to be coming from the cellar window this side,” he told Peter. Peter ran across the fields, David following, and plunged down the steps into the cellar. He staggered back. “Get the long piece of hose from the barn. Run! The wood in the cellar is smouldering.” David dragged the hose back and watched Peter fasten it to the tap of the barrel, tie a wet towel over his mouth and dive into the cellar. “Stay here unless I call,” he told David before he went.

David listened to Peter at work in the cellar. He climbed and looked into the barrel. The water was getting lower and lower. Supposing the barrel ran dry before the fire was out! He would have to carry water from the spring. Should he go and get some? Then he remembered Peter’s words: “Stay here.” Perhaps Peter was afraid of being overcome by the smoke. The hissing grew fainter, and the smoke was not nearly so thick. Then there was silence below. Had Peter fainted? Then he called: “I’m coming.” Peter rolled on the ground, all black, and David wanted to laugh but was afraid he would cry instead. “That was a close shave, David,” said Peter. “It was spontaneous combustion. But how did the barrel get full? There was just enough to the job. I kept thinking – if only the water lasts, I shall save it yet.” “I filled it up this morning,” said David. “Shake hands,” said Peter. “You saved this nice new house of your father’s from burning to a cinder”.

(American prairie story)

| Singing: | Incy Wincy Spider |
| --- | --- |

You will need:

Polystyrene fruit packaging

Scissors

Paint

Paint brush

4 black pipe cleaners – sticks will do

Adhesive tape or masking tape

Transparent red beads

Glue

Darning needle

String

Instructions:

Cut out one of the round shapes from the fruit packaging and paint it black. Allow to dry.

Attach four black pipe cleaners to the underside of the body with adhesive or masking tape to form eight legs on either side of the body. Bend the ends.

Attach two red beads for the eyes.

Use the darning needle to make a hole in the centre of the body. Knot one end of the string and thread the string through the hole.

Suspend Incy Wincy Spider from the roof.

Tips:

Use egg boxes instead of fruit packaging. The spider will be smaller.

Apply glue to the body and sprinkle dried rooibos tea leaves over for a hairy look.

As 10 minutes is all that is allowed for this activity, we suggest the spider’s bodies are pre-cut and pre-painted. All the Cubs need do is assemble their spider.

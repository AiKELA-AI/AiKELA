| Activity: | Floor Collage |
| --- | --- |

Equipment:

Sheets of paper to cover an area of floor

Coloured sand

Flour

Crushed coloured chalk

Fine pebbles

Shells

Pulses (dried peas/legumes)

Any brightly coloured material/paper

Etc.

Broom to sweep up the mess afterwards!

Gather together the above articles (and any others) and spread paper over the floor. Allow the Cubs to work together to create a picture or pattern of their choice. Enjoy the results.

| Yarn: | The First Day of Forever |
| --- | --- |

Our fathers are the fisherman. My father is called Pieter and he works on the Emma, going out to sea on black days when the wind blows and green days when the sea throws the boat around.

Sometimes we fear for him, Ma and Billy and I. All of us – even Billy, who is small – count the heads on the boat to make sure they are all there. And then we look for Pa’s red woolly hat that he always wears. And then our hearts are happy again when we see it.

Today is a blue day, when the sea smiles and we all smile too. Ma and Billy and I went down early to the place where the boats come in. The Emma is sometimes the last boat in, but Pa always says, “slow and steady wins the race in the end.” Then he tells us the story of the hare and the tortoise, which we have heard a hundred times already.

Ma always says that Mrs Hare would not have had as much worry as Mrs Tortoise, but Pa laughs. “Mrs Tortoise always knows her man will come home in the end,” he says. Ma just smiles.

We waited and we waited. The sun was hot, but Ma said it was not as hot on our heads as it was on the heads of the fishermen, out there in the boat.

It got hotter. When would the Emma come home?

And then we saw the first boat, far out beyond the bay.

More people were coming down from the village. This was a special day because it was the first of the yellowtail. Tonight we would eat our favourite fish.

When we look for the boats, when they are just little specks, far out at sea, our eyes stretch and stretch, like they are on elastic. We look and look for our Pa’s boat and we cannot rest until we see it.

This time we saw the colours of the Emma long before the other boats. Billy began to dance up and down, shouting that our Pa’s boat was coming home. I’m older, so I just went to stand with Ma for a bit.

“Okay boys”, Ma said to Bill and me. “You can go and swim now.”

Never will I forget that feeling. Some of the children had a tractor tube. We all carried it down to the beach. I stood in the small waves and shivered a bit, but the others shouted at me and laughed. I walked in, and the water came higher until I was diving through the waves and shouting with the others.

The happiness in my heart grew and grew – like the tractor tyre when we pump it up.

The yellowtail were in. Pa was home safe.

It was the first day of forever.

A story by the children of Arniston – story compiled by Lesley Beake.

| Activity: | Aqua Scope |
| --- | --- |

Equipment: plastic milk bottles; scissors; clear plastic, waterproof tape

Cut the bottom out of the bottle. Cover it with clear plastic and secure with tape. Put the part covered with the plastic in the water and look through the other end to see what is under the water.

If you don’t have a pond of dam nearby, you can use buckets of water into which you have dropped a number of different items for the Cubs to look at.

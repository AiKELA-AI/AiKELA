| Yarn: | A Silly Story |
| --- | --- |

Across the ocean, over lots of huge bumpy mountains, across three hot deserts, and one smaller ocean, there lay the tiny town of Chewandswallow.

There were no food stores in the town of Chewandswallow. They didn’t need any – the sky supplied all the food they could possibly want. The only thing that was really different about Chewandswallow was its weather. It came three times a day, at breakfast, lunch and supper. Everything that everyone ate came from the sky. Whatever the weather served, that was what they ate.

But it never rained. It never snowed; and it never blew just wind. It rained things like soup and juice. It snowed mashed potatoes and green beans. And sometimes the wind blew in storms of hamburgers.

The people could watch the weather report on TV in the morning and they would even hear a prediction of the next day’s food. When the townspeople went outside they carried their plates, cups, glasses, forks, spoons, knives and napkins with them – that way they would always be prepared for any kind of weather.

The menu varied. When they woke up in the morning, breakfast was coming down. After a brief shower of orange juice, low clouds of scrambled eggs moved in, followed by pieces of toast. Butter and jam sprinkled down for the toast and most of the time it rained milk afterwards.

Dinner one night consisted of lamb chops, becoming heavy at times, with occasional ketchup. Periods of peas and baked potatoes were followed by a gradual clearing, with a wonderful fruit jelly, setting in the west.

Life of the townspeople was delicious until the weather took a turn for the worse. One day there was nothing but smelly cheese all day long. The next day there was only overcooked broccoli and the next there Brussels sprouts and peanut butter with mayonnaise. The weather did not improve. One day it rained bread in all shapes and sizes – rolls with and without seeds – lay knee high in the streets and covered the rooftops.

A huge pancake blew in the next day, followed by a downpour of syrup, and covered the school. Things got worse and the public health department could no longer cope with the stale and rotting food lying around.

So a decision was made to abandon the town of Chewandswallow. It was a matter of survival. The people glued together giant pieces of stale bread, sandwich style, with peanut butter. They took only the absolute necessities and set sail for a new land.

Eventually they reached a small coastal town and began their new lives. The biggest change they had to make was getting used to buying food at a supermarket. They found it odd that food was kept on shelves, packed in boxes, cans and bottles and meat had to be cooked and kept in refrigerators.

When they discussed their town of Chewandswallow, many people would ask where they could find this ideal place – they wanted to move straight away. That is until they heard about the bad weather!

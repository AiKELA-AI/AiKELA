| Yarn: | How the Stars Came |
| --- | --- |

The Great Spirit, Gitchi Manito, the Mighty, made the earth and the heaven. There were no lights in the sky at night, so it was dark except on the occasions when the moon shone – sometimes it didn’t. Gitchi Manito, when he had made the earth, went back to the sky. Time went on, and he had almost forgotten the men and women he had made, but it happened they quarreled one night and the sound reached the sky. “What are those earth folk doing?” said Gitchi Manito. He let loose some thunder to let them know he had been disturbed. The awful noise went on; people did not heed the thunder. “What are…doing?” He loosed floods and storms, which fell on the earth. They still went on fighting. “What are…doing? I must see for myself what is the matter.

When Gitchi Manito looked down, he could not see anything at all, because of the dark. “But I must see,” he said. He began to make little holes with his fingers in the darkness and presently could see quite a lot of the earth through the holes. “Oh, dear! dear! Why, they’re all in the dark! No wonder they are quarelling. The earth folk are frightened.” As quickly as he could, he made more holes, ten here, twenty there.

Far below on the earth, a boy called out: “Mother, Father, look, there are tiny lights in the sky.” Father and Mother looked and called their neighbours: “See, see. There are lights, many, many lights in the sky.” Presently, so many of the earth people were gazing at the tiny twinkling lights that the quarrelling ceased, all watching the twinkling of 100,000 stars. In the stillness, Gitchi Manito heard the joyful voice of the boy: “See, see. It is the light of heaven coming through.” “Ah! Not thunder, flood nor storm will stop their quarrelling. It is more light that the earth folk want.” Ever since then the curtain of night remains pierced with stars and on a very starry night earth is always very quiet and still.

(Red Indian story ex There was once…M. Entwistle)

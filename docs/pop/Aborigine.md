## Yarn: The Sun Legend

The following is the yarn text extracted from the attachment. Its cultural provenance is not identified in the source document.

Long ago in the Dreamtime, when the animals were first on the earth, they were much bigger than they are today. There was a time when there was no sun, only a moon and stars.

One day, Dinewan the emu and Brolga the beautiful dancing bird were out on a large plain arguing and fighting. Brolga became so angry that she ran to Dinewan’s nest, grabbed one of her large eggs and threw it into the sky with all her might. It landed on a heap of firewood and broke, spilling the yellow yolk, which burst into flames. This lit up the whole world below. The creatures were astonished, as they had only been used to the semi-darkness and were dazzled by the brightness.

A good spirit who lived in the sky saw how beautiful the earth looked in the blaze. He thought it would be good to make a fire every day, which he has done ever since. All night the good spirit and his helpers collect and stack wood. When the stack is nearly big enough, the good spirit sends out the morning star to let those on earth know that the fire will soon be lit.

However, the spirits found that sending out the morning star was not enough, because those who slept did not see it. They decided there must be a noise at dawn to announce the arrival of the sun and wake the sleepers—but what noise?

Then one day the spirits heard the laughter of Goo-goor-gaga, the kookaburra, ringing through the air. This was the noise they were looking for. They asked Goo-goor-gaga to laugh his loudest every morning as the morning star faded and the day dawned, to awaken everyone before sunrise. Goo-goor-gaga agreed and has done so ever since, making the air ring with his early morning laughter.

Goo-goor-gaga — goo-goor-gaga — goo-goor-gaga.

## Trails work session card

### Tasks in the attachment

The attachment lists “follow a simple trail”, “take part in a scavenger hunt”, making and recognising at least 10 woodcraft signs, following a woodcraft trail, and laying a woodcraft trail using 20 signs for others to follow. The last three statements match the supplied Silver Wolf and Gold Wolf Trail wording as follows:

- **Silver Wolf — Trails:** “Know how to make and recognise at least ten woodcraft signs.” ([requirement sheet, source page 3](../Silver-Wolf-Challenge-Badge-Requirements.md#source-page-3))
- **Silver Wolf — Trails:** “Follow a woodcraft trail.” ([requirement sheet, source page 3](../Silver-Wolf-Challenge-Badge-Requirements.md#source-page-3))
- **Gold Wolf — Trails:** “Lay a woodcraft trail where 20 signs are used for others to follow.” ([requirement sheet, source page 3](../Gold-Wolf-Challenge-Badge-Requirements.md#source-page-3))

“Follow a simple trail” may relate to the Silver Wolf task, but the POP wording does not say that it is a woodcraft trail. The supplied sheets have no matching Trail requirement for taking part in a scavenger hunt. Neither connection is confirmed by the text available.

### Leader preparation

The source does not explain how to set up or run the trail session, or list its equipment. **Scouter suggestion:** prepare a short, clearly bounded route before the meeting, with safe, visible signs and a return point. Decide which task Cubs will practise, show them the signs they need, and let them follow or lay the trail in turn. ## Scouter safety notes

These are practical additions for running the historical activities, not instructions from the POP source:

- **Kangaroo Hunt:** use a soft ball, establish a clear playing boundary, and stop play if Cubs throw above the agreed safe area or crowd one another.
- **Water Relay:** use a non-slip route and place buckets where spilled water will not make a floor slippery.
- **Hungry Birds:** use objects too large to swallow, keep the central area clear, and prohibit pushing or grabbing from another Cub.
- **Kangaroos in Pouches:** clear the circle and hopping route; do not force a Cub through another Cub’s legs, and provide a safe alternative for anyone uncomfortable with that movement.
- **Trails:** keep Cubs within the marked boundary, supervise the route, and take a headcount before and after.

# 1st Pinelands Pack Profile

This file contains Pack-specific planning context for 1st Pinelands. Other Packs
may use the shared POP resources and activity libraries, but must not inherit
these local arrangements unless they confirm that they apply.

This profile is Pack-approved context, not an official Scouts South Africa
rule. Official SSA requirements remain governed by current authoritative SSA
sources.

## Meetings and Programmes

* A programme is normally one Pack meeting on a Friday evening.
* Some programmes have Part 1 and Part 2 and are intentionally run over two
  weeks.
* Each programme has a story that links its elements together.
* Games are important for reinforcing the lesson: “play as we learn” is a
  priority. Some games are included simply for fun and do not need to teach a
  lesson.

## Sixes and Duty Six

* The Pack's Sixes are Red, Yellow, Green and Blue.
* A Duty Six is assigned for each programme and rotates among those four Sixes.
* A Six is a group of about six Cubs.

## Advancement and Term Planning

* For this Pack's planning, Silver Wolf is for Cubs aged 7–8 and Gold Wolf is
  for Cubs aged 9–10. Verify requirements against the SSA advancement sheets.
* The Pack plans on a two-year cycle. Across each cycle, cover every
  advancement at least once in each of the Awareness, Community, Outdoors and
  Aptitude sections, for both Silver Wolf and Gold Wolf.
* A term is about nine meetings. The Pack usually works on a few Interest
  Badges each term as well.
* The usual term allocation is two terms for Aptitude, two for Community, two
  for Outdoors and one for Awareness. Work across these seven terms may
  overlap, and Interest Badge programmes can be added during a term. The eighth
  term in the two-year cycle is reserved for overflow.
* Term dates follow the South African coastal schools calendar. The Pack
  usually does not meet on the first or last Friday of a term, or on a Friday
  that is a public holiday.

## Usual Meeting Sequence

Individual meetings use the same opening and closing structure. A usual
meeting includes, in order:

1. **Grand Howl**, the opening ceremony.
2. A period to learn knots from the Outdoor advancement: one Silver Wolf knot
   and one Gold Wolf knot per term.
3. Inspection.
4. Programme-specific activities and learning.
5. Awarding of badges.
6. Announcements.
7. Bean count.
8. Closing ceremony.

## Bean Count and Golden Rock

Beans are awarded to Sixes for inspection, doing well in games, general
helpfulness and individual acts of goodness. Count beans weekly. The winning
Six is presented with the Golden Rock, a floating trophy. Add weekly bean
totals to determine the term winner and year-end winner.

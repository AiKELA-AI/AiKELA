# 1st Pinelands Pack Profile

This file contains Pack-specific planning context for 1st Pinelands. Other Packs
may use the shared POP resources and activity libraries, but must not inherit
these local arrangements unless they confirm that they apply.

This profile is Pack-approved context, not an official Scouts South Africa
rule. Official SSA requirements remain governed by current authoritative SSA
sources.

## Meetings and Programmes

* A programme is normally one Pack meeting on a Friday evening, scheduled for 17:15–18:45 (90 minutes) using the current Word template.
* Some programmes have Part 1 and Part 2 and are intentionally run over two
  weeks.
* A yarn is optional. When used, the Word plan must be self-contained: include the full yarn and all instructions and equipment needed to run it; do not rely on leaders opening other documents.
* Short read-aloud story snippets may link programme sections. Each should add a new beat to the yarn and lead into the next activity; do not repeat the yarn or reveal an activity's outcome in advance. Put each snippet in an available blank spacer row between the activities it connects, in italics. If more transitions need space, add matching spacer rows to the programme table. Do not leave these passages only in a separate activity guide section.
* Games are important for reinforcing the lesson: “play as we learn” is a
  priority. Some games are included simply for fun and do not need to teach a
  lesson.

## Local document layout

* `templates/00 Cub Program Template.docx` is this Pack’s meeting-plan template.
* `planners/` contains this Pack’s year and term plans.
* `programmes/` contains individual meeting plans and exported programme documents.

Keep these materials in this folder so they are not mistaken for shared resources or applied to another Pack.

## Sixes and Duty Six

* The Pack's Sixes are Red, Yellow, Green and Blue.
* A Duty Six is assigned for each programme and rotates among those four Sixes. For Term 1 2027, start with Blue, then Red, Yellow, Green, and repeat.
* A Six is a group of about six Cubs.

## Advancement and Term Planning

* For this Pack's planning, Silver Wolf is for Cubs aged 7–8 and Gold Wolf is
  for Cubs aged 9–10. Verify requirements against the SSA advancement sheets.
* The Pack plans on a two-year cycle. Across each cycle, cover every
  advancement at least once in each of the Awareness, Community, Outdoors and
  Aptitude sections, for both Silver Wolf and Gold Wolf.
* A term is about nine meetings. The Pack usually works on a few Interest
  Badges each term as well.
* The usual term allocation is two terms for Aptitude, two for Community, two
  for Outdoors and one for Awareness. Work across these seven terms may
  overlap, and Interest Badge programmes can be added during a term. The eighth
  term in the two-year cycle is reserved for overflow.
* Term plans should spread every Silver and Gold criterion across the term by theme, preserve official AND/OR and “do X of Y” wording, and start long-running tasks early. Do not reserve a meeting for reporting or catch-up. Cubs report evidence directly to a Scouter out of band; a parent may send a letter or WhatsApp message for ongoing tasks.
* Term dates follow the South African public-school calendar used by the Pack. The Pack
  usually does not meet on the first or last Friday of a term, or on a Friday
  that is a public holiday.

## Usual Meeting Sequence

Individual meetings use the same opening and closing structure. A usual
meeting includes, in order:

1. **Grand Howl**, the opening ceremony.
2. A period to learn knots from the Outdoor advancement: one Silver Wolf knot
   and one Gold Wolf knot per term.
3. Inspection.
4. A high-energy steam-release game immediately after Inspection.
5. The yarn and programme-specific activities and learning.
6. Awarding of badges.
7. Announcements and bean count.
8. Closing ceremony.

## Bean Count and Golden Rock

Beans are awarded to Sixes for inspection, doing well in games, general
helpfulness and individual acts of goodness. Count beans weekly. The winning
Six is presented with the Golden Rock, a floating trophy. Add weekly bean
totals to determine the term winner and year-end winner.

For 1st Pinelands, use beans as the reward counters throughout the meeting.
When adapting a POP source for this Pack, replace references to “bones” when
they mean reward counters with beans. Do not change a source reference to
actual bones used as an activity object.

## Group-Specific Advancement Activities

When a task is intended for Silver or Gold Cubs separately, put the complete task description in the Activity column and leave the Leader/Group cell blank. The leader of the day fills in group and leader assignments by hand before the meeting. Standard routine items such as knots use “All”; do not preassign programme-specific activities. Keep materials to actual equipment and leave handwriting cells blank rather than filling them with underscores.

## Inspection

Standard inspection items are one blank bullet for later Pack-specific additions, **Knots (2 for each completed)** and **Badges (3/2/1)**. The numbers are beans awarded for that item. Do not add themed or personal inspection checks unless requested.
